PTO & Leave Tracker

A calendar-year leave planner. It tracks vacation accrual, PTO and floating holiday grants, university holidays and the winter closure, then tells you how many hours to use each month so none are lost.

Workdays

Earned hours post at the end of each month. PTO and floating holiday reset on Dec 31. Vacation stops accruing at its cap. Vacation accrual uses your paid hours per week.

Winter closure Hours locked

Holidays and additional paid days need no leave. Every other closure workday is covered with your leave hours, and those hours are set aside so the monthly suggestions never spend them. Check each day's status against the official holiday schedule.

How the suggestions work

  • Vacation accrual: rate × hours paid per week × 52 ÷ 12. At 0.07693 hrs per hour and 40 hrs/week, that's about 13.33 hrs/month, or roughly 160 hrs (20 days) a year. It stops at the 240-hr cap.
  • Use it or lose it (PTO, floating holiday): takes the remaining balance, subtracts logged and locked hours, and spreads the rest over the remaining months of the year. Suggestions even out your total time off each month and never spend locked closure hours.
  • Balance cap (vacation): suggests the minimum to use so the next month's accrual still fits under the cap. You can bank hours up to the cap, then use about one month's accrual each month after that.
  • Cap planner: for each month at the cap, the minimum is rounded up to your chosen increment and placed on open workdays. Holidays, closure days and days you already have off are skipped. Partial days are shown as leaving early. For example, 5.5 hrs on an 8:30–5:00 day with a 30-minute lunch means leaving at 11:00 AM.
  • Work schedule: paid hours per day = end − start − unpaid lunch. With 8:30 AM – 5:00 PM and a 30-minute lunch, that's 8 hrs/day and 40 hrs/week.
  • Winter closure: each closure workday that isn't a holiday or additional paid day needs leave. By default it's covered with use-it-or-lose-it hours first, since they'd be lost on Dec 31 anyway, then with vacation.
